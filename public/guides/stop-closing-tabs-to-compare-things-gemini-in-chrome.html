Stop switching between five tabs to compare things in your head. Chrome has a Gemini button that reads across all your open tabs at once and gives you one answer. Here is how to use it.

↓ Skip to the steps 3 min read · Gemini, Chrome

The mental math you are doing right now

You open three product pages. You read the first one, hold the price and specs in your head, click to the second, try to remember the first, click to the third, and by now you have forgotten something from the first two. So you go back. Twice. Maybe three times.

This is not a focus problem. It is a workflow problem. You are using your short-term memory as a spreadsheet, and short-term memory is terrible at that job.

The shift

The browser already has all the information open. The only missing step was asking it to combine that information for you. Gemini in Chrome is that step.

Step 1
Open your tabs
Products, recipes, flights, job listings. Whatever you would normally compare by switching back and forth.
Step 2
Click the Gemini icon
It lives in the top-right corner of Chrome. One click opens the side panel.
Step 3
Ask your question
Plain language. It reads all the open tabs and replies with one combined answer.

Exactly what to do

  1. Open the tabs you want to compare. Three product pages, three flight options, three recipes, three job postings. However many you would normally have open for a decision like this.
  2. Click the Gemini icon in the top-right corner of Chrome. It opens as a side panel alongside your current tab. You do not need to leave any of the pages you have open.
  3. Type your question in plain language. You do not need special syntax. Ask it the same way you would ask a colleague who had read all three pages.
  4. Read one answer instead of three pages. Gemini pulls the relevant details from each tab and gives you a single, combined response. The comparison happens in the panel, not in your head.

Prompts that actually work

You do not need to write careful prompts for this. Short, direct questions work best. Here are a few worth copying:

Compare tabs
Compare these open tabs and tell me which is the better deal.
Find the difference
What are the main differences between these options?
Specific criteria
Which of these open tabs has the [lowest price / shortest cook time / fewest stops / best benefits]?
Quick summary
Summarise each open tab in one sentence, then recommend one.

Where this saves the most time

The method works anywhere you are holding multiple pages in your head at once. A few situations where it replaces a lot of back-and-forth:

ShoppingThree product pages open. Ask which has the best value for your specific use case, not just the lowest number.
TravelMultiple flight or hotel tabs open. Ask which option has fewer stops, a better cancellation policy, or an earlier arrival.
RecipesA few recipe tabs open. Ask which one uses only the ingredients you already have, or which takes under 30 minutes.
Job listingsSeveral job pages open. Ask which role mentions remote work, or which has the best-described benefits.
ResearchMultiple articles open on the same topic. Ask for a summary of where they agree and where they differ.

The honest bit

  • Gemini in Chrome requires a Google account and the feature to be enabled in your version of Chrome. If you do not see the icon, check that you are on a recent version of Chrome and signed in.
  • It reads page content, not everything behind a login. If a tab requires you to log in and the content is not visible on the page, Gemini may not be able to read it fully.
  • Very long pages may be summarised rather than read in full. For highly detailed comparisons involving dense spec sheets or long contracts, it is worth verifying the key figures yourself.
  • This is a reading and summarising tool, not a price-tracking or real-time data tool. It reads what is on the page at the moment you ask, so prices or availability can change after the page loaded.
  • It is faster for most decisions, not all decisions. For anything with significant financial or legal weight, use this to narrow down your options, then read the finalist yourself.
